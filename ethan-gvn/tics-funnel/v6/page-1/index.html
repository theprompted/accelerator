Three pieces, and the order matters.

1. An outside pathogen gets in

There is a name for this condition: Pediatric Autoimmune Neuropsychiatric Disorders Associated with Streptococcal Infections.

Everyone just says PANDAS, and I will too.

With PANDAS it is usually strep.

Ordinary strep throat, the kind that means a sore throat and a cough for a few days.

In most children the immune system clears it and everybody forgets it happened.

In our children, for whatever reason, the immune system has trouble finishing the job.

Something that ordinary turns into something that reorganizes a family's entire life.

There is no version of washing your hands more carefully that prevents an immune system from misfiring.

2. The immune system misfires, and the brain inflames

What follows is an unusual immune response that causes inflammation through the body, and largely in the basal ganglia, which is the brain's motor cortex.

That inflammation is the actual reason for what you are watching. Loss of motor control. Anxiety. Obsessive thoughts. Compulsions.

Tics that arrive out of nowhere in a child who was fine last month.

For two years my worst tic was hitting myself in the head.

My hand would come down on the desk and my head would twitch toward it, and eventually the two started colliding, over and over, until the skin between my eyebrows swelled up.

I was damaging my own body in front of a classroom and I was completely powerless to stop it.

That is not a discipline problem.

There is no consequence you could have threatened me with that would have worked.

Your child is not behaving this way. Your child's brain is inflamed, and the movements are the smoke.

3. The inflammation drives the symptoms, so the inflammation is the target

This is the piece that reorders everything else.

The infection is the cause. The inflammation is the driver.

Which means there are two jobs, in that order, not one.

First, identify and eliminate the source of infection.

That part is medical, it is usually antibiotics, and it belongs with a doctor.

If you do not clear the infection that is feeding the inflammation, there is no hope of healing.

Second, once that is handled, you go after the inflammation in the brain that is producing the symptoms.

My mother never had this model.
