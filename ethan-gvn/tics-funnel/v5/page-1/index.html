My Mom Spent $56,576 Trying to Stop My Motor Tics. Here's What Finally Worked for Me.

My mom spent eleven years and $56,576 trying to get my tics under control.

Forty doctors, forty different prescriptions.

The medications were not the thing that failed her. They were the thing that made it worse, and on paper they were working.

In tenth grade my math teacher called my parents to say she thought I was on drugs.

She had found me asleep at my desk.

My dad asked me about it that night, and I told him the truth.

Dad, I am on drugs. These medications are killing me. I'm not myself. I can feel my personality fading.

I was quieter. I was not better.

My mom had been paying, for years, to make her son disappear a little more each month.

I know what she was thinking at that kitchen table at two in the morning, because I have asked her.

It was not what is the treatment. It was: what did I do wrong? Did I mess my kid up?

I was thirteen when the first one gave me clonidine.

It is a blood pressure medication.

My blood pressure was fine, but it calmed tremors in some patients, so they tried it on me. Sixty days. Nothing. They raised the dose. Nothing.

My mother was the one who found what it was.

Not a character flaw, not bad parenting, not a mystery.

There is a condition where an infection gets in and the immune system never finishes the fight. Usually it is strep.

Ordinary strep throat, the kind that means a sore throat and a few days off school, the kind most kids clear without anyone thinking about it again.

Some of us cannot close it out.

The immune system keeps swinging at something that is already gone, and the swinging is what does the damage. It inflames the brain.

The basal ganglia, the part that runs motor control.

There is a name for it.

Pediatric Autoimmune Neuropsychiatric Disorders Associated with Streptococcal Infections.

Everyone just says PANDAS, and I will too.

And there is no test for it.

You cannot walk in and ask for the PANDAS test, because it does not exist.

It is blood work and symptom history and old infections and somebody patient enough to assemble the puzzle.

Most doctors do not have that kind of time for one kid.

That is not a conspiracy. That is a waiting room.
