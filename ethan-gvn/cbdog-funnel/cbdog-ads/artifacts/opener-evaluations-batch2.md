## SELECTED EXPRESSIONS WITH FULL EVALUATIONS

### EXPRESSION #2 — Angle 1 (Flat Denial)

**OPENER:** "A tired dog is not a calm dog."
**EVIDENCE PAIRING:** R1 — "Tired dog = calm dog" was never based on science

**TARGET:** The core belief that exhaustion = calm. The belief that wears their body out trying to prove it.

**READER REACTION:** "What? Yes they are. That's literally the advice everyone gives. If my dog is exhausted, they HAVE to calm down."

**GOLD STANDARD:** PASS — This denies a belief they've built their entire routine around. They've structured their mornings and evenings around this. They DEFEND it.

**FUCK YOU TEST:** PASS — Their immediate reaction is "fuck you, I've seen it work" — even though it doesn't last.

**TURN TEST:** PASS — Turn: "Exhaustion isn't peace. It's collapse. The alarm is still running underneath." Natural transition from denial to reframe.

**FAIL PATTERN:** N/A

**VERDICT:** PASS ✓

### EXPRESSION #7 — Angle 2 (Flip)

**OPENER:** "That long walk is feeding the panic, not draining it."
**EVIDENCE PAIRING:** R2 — Studies show cortisol stays elevated after exercise

**TARGET:** Their strategy — using exercise to exhaust the anxiety. The belief that they're doing something helpful.

**READER REACTION:** "Wait, what? I'm making it WORSE? That doesn't make sense. The walk helps!"

**GOLD STANDARD:** PASS — Attacks their solving strategy. They think the walk is medicine. You're telling them it's fuel.

**FUCK YOU TEST:** PASS — They want to argue. They'll think of all the times the dog seemed calmer after a walk.

**TURN TEST:** PASS — Turn: "Physical fatigue and nervous system regulation run on completely different tracks. One is exhausted. One is untouched."

**FAIL PATTERN:** N/A

**VERDICT:** PASS ✓
