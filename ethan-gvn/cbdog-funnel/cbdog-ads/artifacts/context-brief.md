## CORE THESIS

Dog anxiety isn't an energy problem — it's a nervous system problem. Exercise exhausts the body but doesn't reset the alarm system that's stuck in panic mode. The "tired dog is a calm dog" advice is 1970s wisdom that predates our understanding of canine neurochemistry. The dog's nervous system is depleted of what it needs to reset — not overstimulated with excess energy.

**Supporting evidence from funnel:**
1. "A tired dog is a calm dog" comes from the same era as shock collars and dominance theory
2. Researchers measured cortisol after 2-hour walks — it hadn't budged
3. Wild dogs rest 16 hours a day without needing exhaustion to stay calm

## KEY CLAIMS / EVIDENCE SECTIONS

### R1: "Tired Dog = Calm Dog" Was Never Based on Science
- Advice from 1970s, before canine cortisol studies
- First cortisol studies came 20 years later
- Brain scans showing anxiety patterns = 2010s research
- Same era as shock collars and dominance theory

### R2: Studies Show Cortisol Stays Elevated After Exercise
- Researchers measured cortisol after 2-hour walks — hadn't budged
- Physical fatigue and nervous system regulation = completely different tracks
- "The dog was exhausted. The anxiety was untouched."
- Separation anxiety affects 20-40% of dogs

### R3: Wild Dogs Don't Run Marathons to Stay Calm
- Wolves rest up to 16 hours a day
- Wild canids don't have a word for anxiety
- Their alarm fires, danger passes, alarm resets
- They have something in their biology that resets stress automatically

### R4: You Can Walk Miles Every Day and Still Have an Anxious Dog
- Mopping vs. fixing the leak analogy
- Exercise = mopping. Nervous system = leak.
- "Your effort was never the problem. The tool was."
- You can mop forever and lose, or fix the leak once

### R5: Your Dog Isn't Anxious — They're Running Low on Something
- Stuck alarm metaphor (smoke alarm that won't turn off)
- Reset mechanism is starving, not broken
- Every mammal has a built-in stress regulation system
- The nervous system needs something to reset
