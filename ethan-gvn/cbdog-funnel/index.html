⚙ Strategic Rationale

This section explains the why behind the funnel architecture. Understanding the strategy helps you adapt these patterns to future projects.

1 The Funnel Angle ▼

The funnel attacks a specific belief: "Exercise fixes anxiety."

This belief is perfect because it's universal (everyone's heard it), wrong (the science doesn't support it for anxiety), and creates guilt (owners blame themselves when it doesn't work).

By dismantling this belief, we create space for a new one: the dog's nervous system needs support, not exhaustion. This positions CBD as the logical solution—not through features, but through understanding.

2 Level of Awareness ▼

The funnel targets Problem-Aware traffic—people who know their dog has anxiety but don't know the solution.

They're not searching "best CBD for dogs." They're searching "why is my dog still anxious after walks" or "dog anxiety won't go away."

The education page meets them where they are, then moves them up the Awareness Ladder: Problem-Aware → Cause-Aware → Solution-Aware → Product-Aware → Ready to Buy.

3 The 2-Page Architecture ▼

The education page is 100% CBD-free. This isn't an artistic choice—it's compliance architecture.

Meta and Google have restrictions on CBD advertising. By keeping the education page clean, you can run paid traffic directly to it without triggering disapprovals.

The page builds belief and ends with a cliffhanger. The offer page (which contains CBD content) is reached organically—no ads point there directly.

The education page does 80% of the selling. The offer page just has to not mess it up.

4 The Belief Transformation Sequence ▼

The funnel transforms one core belief:

Before: "My dog is anxious because I'm not doing enough."
After: "My dog is anxious because their nervous system needs support."

Five milestones make this shift happen:

  • "The advice I was given was wrong, not me"
  • "Physical exhaustion and mental calm are different systems"
  • "Calm dogs have something anxious dogs don't"
  • "I was mopping when I should have been fixing the leak"
  • "There's a specific system that needs support"

Each milestone makes the next one easier to accept. By the time readers reach the offer page, buying feels like the obvious next step.
