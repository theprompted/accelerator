## Andrew's Ad Account Review

*(Notes from reviewing Ethan's ad account — Feb 27, 2026)*

### Andrew's Response:

Took a look at your stats and not too concerned with a bug or glitch on the spend.

What's happening here is spend that's happening that's being forecasted to drive performance by Meta.

In these cases I like to look at CPM and CPC to see if there's anything unusually high or low but looking at them, they look fine.

And the spend isn't too crazy each day, $229/day for that ad set, which relative to a $60 cost cap is not that high and only allowing for about 4 conversions per day at that cap and budget.

It's also hard to measure definitively what impact the impressions on these ads drive in other campaigns and ad sets.

Which is why I also like looking at the total spend (FB) & sales (Shopify) to see if the overall numbers are concerning.

Running that, you get: $9,856.62 / $3,760.36 = 2.62x ROAS which is decent.

In these cases I usually presume the ads getting spend consistently (which these are) but no sales as having an impact holistically.

I usually don't turn these off outright, instead I'll drop the caps a bit and see if overall performance maintains or improves (overall sales / overall spend).

### Key Metrics Referenced
- **Shopify net sales (Feb 11-16):** $9,856.62
- **Total ad spend (Feb 11-16):** $3,760.36 (Dog Treats Chewables campaign)
- **Blended ROAS:** 2.62x
- **Native Images ad set avg daily spend:** ~$229/day
- **CPM range:** $15-24 (normal)
- **CPC range:** $0.39-$2.87

### Andrew's Framework (for future reference)
1. Don't panic at ad-set-level CPA in isolation
2. Check CPM and CPC for anomalies (glitch indicators)
3. Zoom out to blended ROAS (total Shopify sales / total ad spend)
4. Presume impression-level contribution from ads that spend but don't convert directly
5. Don't kill ad sets outright — drop caps and watch blended performance
