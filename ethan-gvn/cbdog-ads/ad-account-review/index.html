What I'm Seeing

Took a look at your stats and not too concerned with a bug or glitch on the spend.

What's happening here is Meta spending based on its forecast that these ads will drive performance.

In these cases I like to look at CPM and CPC to see if there's anything unusually high or low but looking at them, they look fine.

And the spend isn't too crazy each day, $229/day for that ad set, which relative to a $60 cost cap is not that high and only allowing for about 4 conversions per day at that cap and budget.

It's also hard to measure definitively what impact the impressions on these ads drive in other campaigns and ad sets.

The Blended View

Which is why I also like looking at the total spend (FB) & sales (Shopify) to see if the overall numbers are concerning.

Shopify Net Sales (Feb 11-16) $9,856.62
Total Ad Spend (Feb 11-16) $3,760.36
Blended ROAS 2.62x

Running that, you get: $9,856.62 / $3,760.36 = 2.62x ROAS which is decent.

In these cases I usually presume the ads getting spend consistently (which these are) but no sales as having an impact holistically.

Think of it like a basketball team. Not every player scores 30 points. You need the guy who sets screens and draws the double team so the shooter gets the open look. That ad set's job might not be to close — it might be to warm up the audience so your other ads can.

What I'd Do Next

I usually don't turn these off outright, instead I'll drop the caps a bit and see if overall performance maintains or improves (overall sales / overall spend).

In your case now, since they've been off since Feb 17, I would turn them back on and start with a cap of $40 and work my way back up every 24 hours in $5 increments until spend comes through and is closer to the target you want.

But I wouldn't be too stringent on that one ad set having good metrics — if the overall metrics are still looking good (total sales / total spend) then I'd just leave it as is.

Sometimes you'll have "loser looking" ad sets that are actually just playing a different role on the team — driving awareness and filling the funnel while other ad sets and ads clean up.
