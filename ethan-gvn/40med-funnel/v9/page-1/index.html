I Took 40 Medications for My Motor Tics. Here's What Finally Stopped Them.

I took forty medications for my motor tics. Here is what they cost me.

The money is the easy part to count. My mother spent $56,576 over eleven years.

But beyond the money, the medications cost me something she could not see from the outside.

Why the pills made me quieter but never made me better.

What was actually behind my tics that nineteen doctors missed.

And what I found, years later, that did not just mask the symptoms but let me come back.

What 40 medications actually did to me (it's not what the doctors told my mom)

In tenth grade my math teacher called my parents. She had found me asleep at my desk in the middle of class and she thought I was on drugs.

My dad asked me about it that night.

I told him the truth.

Dad, I am on drugs. These medications are killing me. I'm not myself. I can feel my personality fading.

That was the first time I said it out loud.

The medications were not failing because they did nothing. They were failing because they were aimed at the wrong thing.

Every prescription was treating the smoke, and nobody was looking for the fire.

I was quieter. I was not better.

Quieter child, less child. That was the trade my mother was paying for without knowing it.

My mother had spent years paying for prescriptions that made her son a little easier to manage and a little less like himself with every refill.

At a leadership camp at the University of Virginia my junior year, the other boys were honor students.

They noticed my pills and asked for some. They crushed them up and snorted them.

I was taking those same pills hoping to get back to normal.

I think about that sometimes. What my prescriptions were worth to me and what they were worth to everybody else.

My mother kept a notebook. Fluoxetine tripled in three months. Sertraline doubled. Abilify quadrupled, then she stopped it herself because no doctor would.

She wrote: "There are no improvements with increased dosage. Makes me feel like I'm in a fog and it isn't worth it."

That was my line, in her handwriting. The only person who heard it was the one already staying up past midnight trying to find something that would actually work.

What a tic actually feels like from inside (and why no medication could fix it)

I want to tell you what a tic actually feels like, because this is the part the medications never touched.

From the outside, it looks like a movement he could stop if he tried.

From the inside, it is heat that works its way up your spine, into the back of your neck, and builds until it has to go somewhere.

The pressure does not let up until the movement happens. Then it starts building again.

It is not a thought. It is not a decision. It is physical pressure with one exit.

My dad described it once as ankle weights nobody else could see.

The boy underneath was fine, but he was carrying something on top of himself that made every normal thing harder, and he could not take it off.

I was thirteen, standing at the bathroom counter, trying to put down my toothbrush.

It did not feel right. So I picked it up and put it down again.

Still wrong. Again. Then harder.

Soon I was banging it against the counter, with toothpaste and spit hitting the mirror.

By the time my dad walked in, it looked like I was throwing a fit over a toothbrush.

But inside, I was asking the same question he was: why can't you just put it down?

The medications were numbing me. They were not taking the ankle weights off.

They were just making me too tired to notice them, and too flattened to do anything about it.

Why my tics disappeared during baseball and drums (this changes everything)

I played drums. I played baseball.

During those activities, for the full time I was doing them, I had zero tics. Not fewer. Zero.

Same brain. Same kid. Same day.

If you have watched your child be completely calm during one activity and out of control during another, you have probably been told that proves he could stop if he tried.

It does not prove that.

It proves the brain works fine when the load is off.

Something else is sitting on top of it, and that something responds to conditions.

The ankle weights come off during certain activities. That is data.

The medications were aimed at the brain. The brain was not the problem.

The weight on top of it was.

The question every mother asks herself at 2am (and why the answer is not what you think)

I know what my mother asked herself at that kitchen table at two in the morning, because I have asked her.

It was not what is the treatment. It was: what did I do wrong? Did I mess my kid up?

She carried that question for eleven years, and no doctor in eleven years ever took it off her.

Right behind it came the other one: something is not right. You look at your child and you know with total certainty this is not who he is.

He is a different person.

And nobody can tell you what happened.

My mother went looking for the answer in herself. What she fed me. What she missed. Whether she should have caught it sooner.

My mom never gave up hope. We kept trying, but I gave up hope. She did not.

She was looking in the wrong place.

Not because she was careless.

Because nobody had handed her anywhere else to look.

You did not cause this.

I cannot tell you what is happening inside your son. No page can.

But I can tell you what my family finally found after eleven years, and it was not a parenting mistake. It was a mechanism.

What 19 doctors missed that my mom found on her own

The doctors had a word for me. Tourette's.

Sometimes anxiety, sometimes behavioral.

I do not think they were evil and I do not think they were lazy.

I think they had ten minutes with me and a prescription pad, and a word on paper is what ten minutes buys you.

Something else was going on underneath it.

My mother was the one who found what it was.

There is a condition where an infection gets in and the immune system never finishes the fight. Usually it is strep.

Ordinary strep throat, the kind that means a sore throat and a few days off school, the kind most kids clear without anyone thinking about it again.

Some of us cannot close it out.

The immune system keeps swinging at something that is already gone, and the swinging is what does the damage. It inflames the brain.

The basal ganglia, the part that runs motor control.

There is a name for it.

Pediatric Autoimmune Neuropsychiatric Disorders Associated with Streptococcal Infections.

Everyone just says PANDAS, and I will too.

And there is no test for it.

It is blood work and symptom history and old infections and somebody patient enough to assemble the puzzle.

Most doctors do not have that kind of time for one kid.

That is not a conspiracy. That is a waiting room.

The medications were aimed at the smoke. Something was burning underneath them.

And once you know something is burning, the order of operations changes.

You clear the infection first, and that is a doctor's job, usually antibiotics.

Then you go after the inflammation it left behind in the brain, because that is what is producing the symptoms you are watching.

Forty medications treated the smoke. Not one was aimed at the fire.

The prescription that proved the pharmaceutical version doesn't work

One of those forty medications was called Marinol.

It is a synthetic cannabinoid, a pharmaceutical copy of the active compound in marijuana, made in a lab and dispensed by prescription.

This was not alternative medicine. This was a prescription from a doctor, covered by the system, delivered by a pharmacy.

After thirty days of taking it, nothing. Absolutely nothing.

My mother's notebook confirms it independently. In her handwriting: "Ethan doesn't see any initial improvement."

The pharmaceutical version of the plant did not work.

Years later, the plant itself did.

I did not understand why at the time. I do now. But that is the next part of this story.

The one treatment that works for inflammation (and why your child can't stay on it)

I want to be fair, because my mother deserved fairness on this.

Antibiotics to clear the infection are valid and necessary.

Anti-inflammatory steroids do help.

But nobody should be on steroids longer than about thirty days.

The side effects are tremendous and can be devastating.

So look at the corner this puts a mother in.

The inflammation needs to come down and stay down, for months.

The tool that does it best is the one her child has to stop taking after a month.

What my mom was really searching for at that kitchen table

That gap is what my mother was searching for at that kitchen table. She just did not have a word for it.

You already know the shape of it, even if nobody has ever said it back to you.

Not one more thing to add to the pile. You have a pile.

You have the magnesium and the diet change and the specialist eight months out.

You are the one who has to decide, every single time, whether the next thing is worth what it might cost him.

What you have been looking for is something that goes after what is actually driving this.

That he can stay on longer than a month.

And that does not hand you back a quieter kid who is somehow less himself.

Nobody told my mother that existed.

It took me until my twenties to find it, and I found it the worst possible way.
