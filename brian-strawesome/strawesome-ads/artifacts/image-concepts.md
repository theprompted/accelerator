## FULL SELF-CHECK EVALUATIONS

### CONCEPT 1: "Ice on Metal"

**CONCEPT:** Ice on Metal
**DESCRIPTION:** A metal straw with visible frost/condensation sticking out of a Stanley tumbler with iced coffee, the straw looks painfully cold
**SUBJECT TYPE:** Object — no people

**CONTRADICTION:** Evidence of Failure
The frost on the straw IS the problem. A drinking tool shouldn't look like a frozen pipe. The visual says "this will hurt your teeth" without needing text.

**LAYER 1 (TECHNICAL):** Would someone take this photo with their phone?
Yes. Someone noticing their straw is frosted over while sitting at their desk or in their car would grab a quick snap. Lighting: fluorescent office light or harsh car interior sun. Imperfect: slightly tilted, one-handed grab shot.

**LAYER 2 (CONTEXT):** Would someone post this?
Yes. Implied caption: "my straw is literally frozen solid rn" or "tell me it's Monday without telling me it's Monday" or "my teeth hurt just looking at this"

**LAYER 3 (INTENT):** Do you feel something before any text?
Yes. Recognition/wincing. Anyone who's used a metal straw with ice drinks knows this feeling — the cold metal against teeth. The image triggers that memory before any text.

**OPENER MATCH:** Ad 1 — "You still wince when the hot coffee hits your lips."
The opener is about temperature discomfort you've learned to live with. The image shows the COLD version of that same problem. Match is EMOTIONAL STATE (persistent discomfort recognition), not just TOPIC (metal straws).

**FACE TEST:** No face. N/A.

**STOCK CHECK:** Could this appear in a stock library?
No. Stock has "Stanley tumbler product shot" and "condensation on glass." Not a phone-quality shot of a frosted-over metal straw in an imperfect setting. The specific ugly (frost pattern, casual angle) isn't stock.

**AI TEXT CHECK:** Does this require readable text?
No. No text elements in the concept. The frost pattern tells the story.

**IMMEDIATE CONTEXT CHECK:** Would a stranger identify the problem space in 3 seconds?
Yes. Visual cues: Stanley tumbler (recognizable), metal straw, frost/condensation, iced drink. Anyone with a tumbler recognizes this immediately.

**VERDICT:** PASS

### CONCEPT 5: "Mason Jar Spike"

**CONCEPT:** Mason Jar Spike
**DESCRIPTION:** A mason jar with a metal lid and metal straw inserted through the lid hole, the straw pointing straight up vertically like a spike
**SUBJECT TYPE:** Object — no people

**CONTRADICTION:** "Wait, What?" Detail
The setup looks cute/normal — mason jar drinking glasses are trendy. But the straw is held rigidly in place by the lid. The vertical spike-like position creates unease. The "cute" setup is actually the dangerous setup from the coroner's warning.

**LAYER 1 (TECHNICAL):** Would someone take this photo with their phone?
Yes. Kitchen counter, someone made themselves a drink in their cute mason jar, quick snap. Overhead fluorescent or window light.

**LAYER 2 (CONTEXT):** Would someone post this?
Yes in an innocent way (before awareness). Implied caption: "my aesthetic" or "mason jar life." After reading the ad, the image transforms from cute to ominous.

**LAYER 3 (INTENT):** Do you feel something before any text?
The vertical spike position is unusual and slightly unsettling. Most straw photos show straws at drinking angles, not pointing straight up. The rigidity of the setup (lid holding straw firmly) adds to the unease.

**OPENER MATCH:** Ad 5 — "The problem was never the material that breaks."
The opener is about inverted risk — worrying about the wrong variable. The image shows the "safe" setup that's actually dangerous. The lid that "conveniently" holds the straw is the hazard. Match is EMOTIONAL STATE (realizing you've been worrying about the wrong thing).

**FACE TEST:** No face. N/A.

**STOCK CHECK:** Could this appear in a stock library?
Partially — "mason jar with straw" exists in stock. BUT: the specific angle emphasizing the straw-as-spike, the tight lid grip, phone-quality imperfection — that's not stock. The ominous framing isn't how stock shoots this.

**AI TEXT CHECK:** Does this require readable text?
No. The setup itself tells the story.

**IMMEDIATE CONTEXT CHECK:** Would a stranger identify the problem space in 3 seconds?
They'd recognize it as a drink setup. The specific danger requires context from the ad copy. However, the vertical spike position creates subliminal unease that pairs powerfully with the opener.

**VERDICT:** PASS (works with opener to transform meaning)
