## SELECTED EXPRESSIONS WITH SELF-CHECK EVALUATIONS

### Expression #17 (Angle 5: Quit Proof, Evidence: R1)

**OPENER:** "You've been using metal straws for years. You still wince when the hot coffee hits your lips. That's not user error."

**TARGET:** Their lived experience with metal straws — specifically, the discomfort they've normalized

**READER REACTION:** "Wait... I DO wince. I've been blaming myself or just accepting it. Are you saying the straw is the problem?"

**GOLD STANDARD:** PASS — Attacks their acceptance of discomfort as normal. Names a bodily experience they recognize but dismissed. Feels personal because it IS personal — it's describing their morning.

**FUCK YOU TEST:** PASS — The reader's first instinct isn't to agree politely. It's "How do you know that?" or "That's not... wait, is that true?" The wince is undeniable. The reframe creates friction.

**TURN TEST:** PASS
Turn: "Metal conducts every degree. Hot pan handle energy, straight into your lips. The problem was never your technique."

**FAIL PATTERN:** N/A

**VERDICT:** PASS

### Expression #31 (Angle 8: Protect, Evidence: R2)

**OPENER:** "Your suspicion that you can't really clean the inside was right. You can't see through metal. You're trusting a kitchen you've never inspected."

**TARGET:** Their private doubt about hygiene — the quiet suspicion they've been ignoring

**READER REACTION:** "I've... actually thought about this. I DO wonder if it's really clean. You're saying my doubt was correct?"

**GOLD STANDARD:** PASS — Validates a doubt while simultaneously surfacing it. The reader didn't want to think about this. Now they have to.

**FUCK YOU TEST:** PASS — Not "fuck you, that's wrong" but "fuck you for making me think about this." The reader can't unsee the metaphor. They feel exposed.

**TURN TEST:** PASS
Turn: "You weren't being paranoid. Bacteria, biofilm, mold — all invisible in an opaque tube. Your instinct to doubt was protecting you."

**FAIL PATTERN:** N/A

**VERDICT:** PASS

### Expression #13 (Angle 4: Attack Trade-Off, Evidence: R3)

**OPENER:** "You traded breakable for unbreakable. What you actually got was 'something else breaks instead.'"

**TARGET:** Their entire decision framework — the logic of "unbreakable = safe"

**READER REACTION:** "Wait, what? I chose unbreakable ON PURPOSE. What do you mean something else breaks? ...Oh. You mean my teeth."

**GOLD STANDARD:** PASS — Attacks the frame of the original purchase decision. The reader made a PROUD choice ("I went with metal because it can't break"). This opener says that choice was a trap.

**FUCK YOU TEST:** PASS — The reader wants to defend their decision. "No, unbreakable IS better." Then the second sentence lands and they have to stop and think.

**TURN TEST:** PASS
Turn: "Starbucks recalled 2.5 million metal straws after mouth lacerations. Metal doesn't give on impact — your teeth do."

**FAIL PATTERN:** N/A

**VERDICT:** PASS

### Expression #22 (Angle 6: Real Variable, Evidence: R5)

**OPENER:** "The problem was never the material that breaks. It was the material that won't."

**TARGET:** Their core assumption — that breakability is the problem to solve

**READER REACTION:** "What? Breaking IS bad. That's why I chose metal. ...Wait, are you saying the REAL danger is something else?"

**GOLD STANDARD:** PASS — Inverts their entire mental model. The thing they were avoiding (breakability) was never the risk. The thing they chose (rigidity) is.

**FUCK YOU TEST:** PASS — Immediate cognitive dissonance. "That can't be right. Of course breaking is bad." Then the turn makes them reconsider.

**TURN TEST:** PASS
Turn: "When glass breaks, it crumbles. When metal doesn't break, something else does. A coroner issued a warning about exactly that."

**FAIL PATTERN:** N/A

**VERDICT:** PASS
