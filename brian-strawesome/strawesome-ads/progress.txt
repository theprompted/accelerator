- Replaced with Expression #26 (Angle 7: Guilt, R4) which passed all 3 tests
- Saved full evaluations to artifacts/opener-evaluations.md

**Learnings for future iterations:**
- "Science" angle expressions (Angle 10) are high-risk for the observation fail pattern — they inform rather than disrupt
- When an expression presents a factual statement (chromium leaches, Starbucks recalled, etc.) without implicating the READER, it fails the gold standard test
- The successful expressions all name something the reader has EXPERIENCED or FELT — wince, suspicion, doubt, metallic taste, trade-off they made
- Expressions that start with "Your..." or "You've..." naturally pass because they speak to lived experience
- Expressions that start with facts/numbers/research naturally fail because they educate

**Key insight:** The distinction isn't topic (all 40 expressions are about metal straws). The distinction is whether the READER is the subject of the sentence or the metal straw is.

- Concept 5 (mason jar spike) is borderline standalone but transforms powerfully WITH the opener. The vertical spike position creates subliminal unease that the copy names.
- Specific ugly in prompts: "autofocus locked on background" beats "slightly blurry"; "single overhead fluorescent making everything yellow-green" beats "harsh lighting"

- Ad 3 (Attack Trade-Off): Shows damage despite "unbreakable" → validates trade-off regret
- Ad 4 (Guilt): Shows visible degradation → validates sensory experience (if it's changing outside, what about inside?)
- Ad 5 (Real Variable): Shows the "safe" setup that's dangerous → validates inverted risk
