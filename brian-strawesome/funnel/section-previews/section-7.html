5. When Metal Straws Fail, They Fail Dangerously

"Unbreakable" becomes "unforgiving" the moment something goes wrong.

In November 2018, Elena Struthers-Gardner — a 60-year-old retired jockey in England — fell while carrying a Mason jar with a metal straw.

The straw impaled her through her eye socket.

It pierced her brain.

She died two days later.

The coroner issued a warning: metal straws should not be used with lids that hold them in place.

On impact, a metal straw becomes a weapon.

Think about car safety design.

Modern cars have crumple zones — parts designed to fail in a specific way.

Metal straws have no crumple zones.

The safest tools aren't the ones that never fail — they're the ones that fail safely.

You shouldn't have to accept increased injury risk for reusability.

There's a material that handles impact differently.

One that's been engineered to be both durable AND safe.
