# Analysis: Nadja's 20 Angles vs. Native Ad Requirements

**Date:** March 12, 2026
**Analyst:** Andrew + Claude
**Context:** Stefan shared Nadja's 20 LLM-generated ad angles. We need to assess which are actually usable for native image ads.

---

## The Core Problem

**Nadja's angles are informational. Native ads need to be confrontational.**

"Confrontational" doesn't mean aggressive — it means **attention-grabbing through violated expectations**. If you say things people expect, it becomes elevator music. If you say things people don't expect, you capture attention to then make your point.

The difference:
- **Informational:** Reader learns something, nods, scrolls
- **Confrontational:** Reader feels a flash of "wait, what?" or mild defensiveness, stops to resolve the tension

---

## The LP Already Does the Work Nadja's Angles Are Attempting

Stefan's landing page (matandvics.com/pages/lp01) opens with:

> **"Warum die weichsten Socken die schlechtesten für deine Füße sind"**
> (Why the softest socks are the worst for your feet)

And the core accusation:

> **"Du fällst auf den Weichheits-Trick herein. Jedes. Einzelne. Mal."**
> (You fall for the softness trick. Every. Single. Time.)

The LP's structure:
- **3 Sekunden:** Feels soft in store → purchase decision
- **3 Stunden:** Feet sweating, sock feels damp
- **3 Wochen:** Holes, shape lost, need to replace

**The LP attacks a belief ("soft = comfortable") and resolves it with the 78/22 formula.**

---

## The Role Confusion in Nadja's Angles

Most of Nadja's angles are **re-explaining what the LP already teaches** instead of **creating a scroll-stop that makes someone click to the LP**.

| What the Ad Should Do | What the LP Should Do |
|-----------------------|----------------------|
| Attack a belief | Resolve the tension |
| Create cognitive dissonance | Provide the education |
| Make them feel caught/curious | Deliver the proof |
| Stop the scroll | Close the sale |

Nadja's angles blur this line. They're trying to educate in the ad instead of attacking in the ad and educating on the LP.

## The Transformation Required

For the 45% that are "informational," we need to run them through the violation framework:

**Example transformation:**

| Original (Informational) | Transformed (Confrontational) |
|-------------------------|------------------------------|
| "We tested 100+ sock prototypes" | "Your sock drawer is full of marketing. Mine is full of math." |
| "I did the math on what my €5 socks cost me" | "The 'cheap' sock costs more. You just pay in installments." |
| "Why do feet actually smell?" | "Your feet don't smell. Your socks trap the bacteria that do." |
| "At 3pm you feel it" | "Your socks gave up at noon. You're feeling it now." |

The transformation shifts:
- From **describing** → to **accusing**
- From **reader learns** → to **reader feels caught**
- From **curiosity** → to **cognitive dissonance**

---

## The 3-Test Quality Gate

Every expression must pass:

### Test 1: Gold Standard Test
> Reading ONLY the first line — would someone think: "Wait, are you attacking something I believe?"

**PASS:** Reader feels defensive.
**FAIL:** Reader nods along.

### Test 2: "Fuck You" Test
> Does the first line make the reader think "fuck you, that's not true" BEFORE they read line 2?

**PASS:** Triggers defensiveness, instinct to argue.
**FAIL:** Reader feels informed or educated.

### Test 3: Turn Test
> Can you turn it within 2-3 lines so the reader realizes you're on their side?

**PASS:** Natural turn — "Oh, you're not attacking ME. You're attacking the thing that failed me."
**FAIL:** Turn feels manipulative or takes too long.

## The Prompt Problem

Nadja used this prompt across Gemini, ChatGPT, and Claude:

> "This is the landingpage for our Meta campaign to drive traffic to sell our Mat & Vic's premium socks: [URL]. Pls analyze the page and suggest 10 themes/angles for new ads (TOFU, Style: native ads)"

**Why this prompt produces informational angles:**
1. "Analyze the page" → LLMs extract information and repackage it
2. "Suggest themes/angles" → LLMs describe topics, not violations
3. "Native ads" → LLMs know the format but not the psychology
4. No instruction to attack beliefs or create cognitive dissonance

**Better prompt structure would include (for Nadja's future use):**
- What belief should the reader feel attacked?
- What should the reader think before they scroll past?
- What tension should they need to resolve by clicking?

## Recommendation

**Don't generate images for all 20 angles.**

1. Use the 5 confrontational angles (2, 12, 13, 17, 20) as-is
2. Transform the 9 informational angles through violation framework
3. Discard the 6 brand messaging angles entirely

**The batch 2 work we did on March 11 already attempted this transformation.** But reviewing those expressions with fresh eyes, some are borderline — they sound edgy but don't actually create defensiveness.

**Next step:** Review the batch 2 expressions against this analysis and sharpen the weak ones before generating images.

---

## Key Principle

**The ad's job is to create cognitive dissonance. The LP's job is to resolve it.**

Nadja's angles try to do both in the ad. That's why they feel informational — they're explaining instead of attacking.

The ad should make someone feel caught, confused, or mildly defensive. The LP catches them and says "let me explain why you've been tricked — and here's the solution."
