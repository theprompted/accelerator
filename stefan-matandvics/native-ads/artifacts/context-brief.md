## KEY CLAIMS / EVIDENCE SECTIONS

### R1: "Natural" Doesn't Mean Better — It Means Designed For Animals
- Appeal to Nature Fallacy — natural doesn't equal good
- Alpaca fiber evolved for 15,000 feet in the Andes, standing still in freezing temps
- Merino wool evolved for Scottish rain, sheep grazing in open fields
- "Natural" solutions to problems you don't have
- "It's like expecting a fish to climb a tree"

### R2: The Softest Sock At 9am Is The Sweatiest Sock By 5pm
- Softness ≠ comfort
- Natural fibers absorb moisture and hold onto it
- Feet produce half a pint of sweat per day
- "Like wearing a down comforter in July"
- Comfort is a system: moisture, temperature, all-day performance

### R3: "Special Care Required" Is An Admission Of Design Failure
- Care instructions are a confession, not a feature
- "High-maintenance isn't the same as high-quality"
- Cold water only, gentle cycle, lay flat to dry, no fabric softener...
- "It's like buying a sports car that only works on perfectly smooth roads"
- Miss one step and €50 alpaca socks felt into slippers

### R4: Premium Fibers Wear Out Faster Than You Think
- Wool pills (fiber literally falling apart from friction)
- Alpaca felts (one wrong wash, fibers mat together permanently)
- Cashmere thins (ultra-fine = weak)
- "Perceived durability isn't the same as actual durability"
- Polyamide resists abrasion — why hospitals and military use it

### R5: "100% Merino" Sounds Premium — It's Actually A Compromise
- 100% anything inherits all weaknesses with no compensation
- "Purity isn't the same as optimization"
- "It's like building a house entirely from glass"
- Best buildings use multiple materials for different jobs
- The chemistry equation (78/20/2) is what actually works
