Two Numbers That Determine Everything

Before we talk about ads, funnels, or creative — we need to talk about math.

There are two numbers that determine whether paid acquisition will work for you:

Number 1: Gross Margin

Gross margin is what's left after you pay for the product and get it to the customer.

It's revenue minus COGS, shipping, payment fees, and fulfillment.

This is what you have available to spend on ads and still make money.

The benchmarks:

50% gross margin — minimum to make paid ads viable

60% gross margin — healthy, gives you room to compete

70%+ gross margin — ideal, lets you outbid competitors and still profit

Your current gross margin on a single 10-pack is about 49%.

Here's the breakdown:

Gross Sales (incl. VAT) €27.99
VAT (19%) −€4.47
COGS −€6.10
Shopify Fees (4%) −€1.12
Shipping/Pick/Pack (net of customer contribution) −€2.38
JTL Fee −€0.20
Gross Margin €13.72 (49%)

Imagine you signed up for a swim race.

You trained hard. You studied technique. You optimized your nutrition.

And every race, you're still coming in last.

So you train harder. Wake up earlier. Refine your stroke.

Still last.

At some point, someone pulls you aside and says:

"Hey... you know you're wearing ankle weights, right?"

And you look down, and there they are.

No amount of training fixes ankle weights.

You can have perfect technique. You can want it more than anyone else in the pool.

The weights don't care.

The answer isn't to swim harder.

The answer is to take off the weights.

A 49% gross margin on a single-pack order is ankle weights.

You can optimize your ads. You can build better funnels. You can test more creatives.

But the math is the math.

Number 2: Allowable CPA

Allowable CPA is how much you can spend to acquire a customer and still hit your profit target.

This is different from break-even CPA — it's what you can afford while keeping 20% contribution margin.

The reality of ad auctions:

Meta and Google are auctions. The brands that can afford to pay more for a customer get more impressions, more clicks, more conversions.

If your allowable CPA is €12 and your competitor's is €30, they will outbid you for every valuable impression.

You don't just lose some auctions. You lose most of them.

I want your allowable CPA to be €30 or higher.

Not because I want you to spend more.

Because I want you to be competitive in the auction.

Why We're Not Starting With The Shop Audit

A shop audit first makes sense when you're doing 20-100 orders a day.

At that volume, a 5% conversion rate improvement is meaningful. You have traffic. You have data. Small fixes compound into real money.

When you're not there yet, optimizing the shop is like polishing the cash register before customers walk in the door.

It feels productive. But it's not the bottleneck.

Your website is technically sound.

It's a strong container with weak components inside.

The container doesn't need rebuilding.

The components — the messaging, the Thesis, the reason to buy — need strengthening.

The bottleneck isn't a better site.

The bottleneck is unit economics and Thesis that let you drive traffic profitably.

That's where we start.

You're Not in the Sock Business

Mat & Vic's is not a sock company.

You're not selling fabric, elasticity, or thread count.

You're not here to convince people the cotton is soft or the seam won't irritate.

That's commodity talk.

That's how people sell when they don't have a perspective.

Every brand that competes on specs ends up in the same fight: who can say "premium quality" louder, who can offer a better bundle, who can slash prices first.

That race has no winners.

What you're actually selling is a reframe.

A new way for someone to think about a decision they've already made a hundred times.

That's what a Thesis does.

What Is a Thesis?

A Thesis is the core perspective or belief that underlies everything your brand says.

It's not a tagline.

It's not a slogan.

It's the answer to: "What does this brand believe about the world that changes how someone sees their options?"

When a Thesis is strong, it shifts how the customer evaluates their choices.

They stop comparing you to competitors on the same criteria.

They start seeing you as the obvious choice for a different reason entirely.

Without a Thesis, you're left competing on features.

And features are a race to the bottom.

Why Thesis Beats Specs

If you don't go the Thesis route, here's what happens:

You fall into Alibaba-style marketing.

You end up saying things like:

  • "Premium European cotton!"
  • "Öko-Tex certified!"
  • "10-pack value bundle!"

None of that is a real edge.

It's generic. It's forgettable.

It puts you in the same bucket as every other sock brand scrambling for attention in a crowded feed.

When brands lean into specs, margins thin, growth slows, and customers don't stick.

But when a brand shifts to Thesis-driven marketing, everything accelerates.

Because people aren't buying the product.

They're buying the perspective.

Quantity per order goes up — once someone sees themselves in your worldview, they want more of it.

Ad performance improves — the messaging is magnetic, not transactional.

Specs are a race to the bottom. Thesis is how you win without playing someone else's game.

How Thesis Informs Everything Downstream

Here's what changes when you have a Thesis locked in:

Ads become easier.

You're no longer guessing at angles. Every ad is an expression of the same core belief. Some are louder, some are softer — but they all pull from the same source.

Website copy writes itself.

Headlines, product descriptions, about pages — all flow from the same perspective. No more staring at a blank page wondering what to say.

Creative direction gets clearer.

Every design decision has a filter. You're not guessing at what "on-brand" means — the Thesis defines it.

Upsells and bundles make sense.

You're not just offering "more socks." You're offering more of the identity the customer already bought into.

What a Thesis Looks Like

A Thesis is a core perspective or belief that underlies all your marketing.

It's not a tagline or a slogan.

It's the answer to: "What does this brand believe about the world that changes how someone sees their options?"

Here are examples from other brands:

Jones Road Beauty ($100M+/year):

Commodity: Wax-based balms, castor oil, jojoba seed oil

Thesis: "Makeup should enhance your face, not cover it up."

Suddenly the product isn't just "clean makeup." It's rebellion against heavy filters, full coverage, and overdone beauty standards.

HexClad ($100M+/year):

Commodity: Nonstick pans with stainless steel layer

Thesis: "You don't need to choose between convenience and performance."

The idea turns a shiny piece of cookware into a category of one. They resolved a hidden tension people didn't know they were allowed to challenge.

Jolie Showerhead:

Commodity: Filtered showerhead

Thesis: "Your shower water is the hidden enemy of your skin and hair."

The problem wasn't your moisturizer. It was your plumbing. A complete reframe of what's causing the problem.

DadGang ($10M+/year, team of 3):

Commodity: Baseball caps with prints

Thesis: "Dads deserve better than the dad-joke stuff you find on Etsy."

They aren't selling hats. They're selling dignity and identity for a forgotten segment of men. A movement disguised as merch.

Thesis Examples For Mat & Vic's

Below are Thesis directions to jumpstart our thinking together.

These aren't finished positions to pick from.

They're starting points for the back-and-forth we'll do in Weeks 1-2 to land on something that feels right for you and the brand.

Option 1: The Engineered Blend

Common belief: "Natural is better than synthetic."

Reframe: Natural fibers evolved for animals. Polyamide was engineered for humans.

"Alpaca fiber evolved to keep alpacas warm at 15,000 feet. Merino wool evolved to protect sheep from Scottish rain. Neither was designed for your feet.

Polyamide was invented specifically to solve human problems: durability under stress, rapid moisture transfer, resistance to wear.

Our 78% cotton / 20% polyamide blend isn't a compromise—it's a collaboration."

Option 2: The Washing Machine Truth

Common belief: "Premium socks are worth the investment."

Reframe: A sock you can't throw in any wash isn't premium—it's a liability.

"Here's what alpaca sock companies don't mention: special washing instructions.

Cold water only. Gentle cycle. Lay flat to dry. No fabric softener. Miss one step? Your €50 socks felt into slippers.

Our cotton-polyamide blend goes in with your jeans, your towels, your whatever. Hot water. Cold water. Tumble dry. It doesn't care."

Option 3: The Daily Driver

Common belief: "I should invest in quality socks."

Reframe: The sock you wear every day matters more than the one you save for special occasions.

"You don't drive a Ferrari to the grocery store. You don't wear a tuxedo to your kid's soccer game.

So why would you wear €50 alpaca socks to the office?

Your daily life needs socks designed for reality. Socks that perform under pressure, wash without drama, and cost little enough that you own plenty of them.

Alpaca is an event. Cotton-polyamide is a lifestyle."

Why Funnels Matter More Than Your Product Page

The schedule shows "Build ads and funnels" in Weeks 3-6.

Here's why funnels are central to the plan.

Your Shopify product page does one thing: show the product and let people buy it.

It doesn't teach.

It doesn't reframe.

It doesn't do the Thesis work.

A funnel — sometimes called a landing page or advertorial — is different.

It's a dedicated page built for one purpose: to shift how someone thinks before they see the price.

Think of it this way.

A product page is like a shelf in a store.

It displays what you're selling. It assumes people already want it.

A funnel is like a conversation with a trusted friend.

It explains why this matters. It answers the objections they haven't voiced yet. It earns the right to ask for the sale.

The shelf converts browsers. The conversation creates buyers.

Hollow Socks: The Model That Works

Hollow Socks is a US-based alpaca sock brand.

They've sold over 2 million pairs. They have 10,000+ five-star reviews.

They compete at $34-70 per pair — 10-20x the price of commodity socks.

Here's what's notable:

They didn't initially build their business with fancy video ads or viral UGC content.

They built it with static image ads that drive to landing pages.

Their landing pages do the heavy lifting.

They explain why alpaca fiber is different.

They create a worldview where premium pricing makes sense.

They handle objections before the customer reaches checkout.

By the time someone sees the product page, the sale is already 80% made.

The page just closes it.

The lesson: You don't need expensive video production or influencer content to scale on Meta.

You need a Thesis that lands — and a funnel that teaches it.

What This Means For Mat & Vic's
