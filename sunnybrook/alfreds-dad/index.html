Alfred's Dad — Open Heart Surgery Experience

Alfred's dad had a CABG (coronary artery bypass grafting). Miranda is having mitral valve replacement + tricuspid valve repair + possible maze procedure, but many of the recovery dynamics are similar.

The Surgery

  • Check-in to OR: ~6-7 hours of waiting
  • Surgery itself: ~4-5 hours
  • Surgery was deemed successful

Post-Op Complications

Mini-Stroke (24 hours post-op)

  • Happened while family was visiting in the CCU
  • Suddenly started speaking gibberish, blood pressure dropped, nurses rushed in
  • CT scan confirmed mini-stroke
  • Doctors said this is not uncommon after open-heart surgery and often has no long-term effects
  • At the time: very scary, "wait and see" situation

Prostate Complication (unrelated to heart)

  • Ongoing bleeding in urine → repeated catheter insertions (very painful)
  • Dropping hemoglobin → blood pressure crashes
  • Caused: dizziness, disorientation, hallucinations, not recognizing family, speaking nonsense
  • All of this was due to blood loss — but they didn't know that at the time
  • Heart itself was healing well throughout — cardiac side was actually fine

Falls on the Ward

  • Fell multiple times — once found passed out on bathroom floor in the middle of the night
  • Each fall meant CT scans to check for head injury
  • Family had to intervene and demand constant supervision
  • When blood pressure dropped, he would try to get up unsafely — had to override his decisions (emotionally very hard)
  • After second fall: spoke with ward manager → got a private room
  • Alfred stayed overnight with him for about a week

Resolution

  • Took ~3 weeks to determine prostate was the root cause
  • Multiple specialists involved: cardiology, neurology, urology
  • After discharge: collapsed again at home weeks later, same symptoms, ambulance back to ER
  • Operated on the prostate → that finally fixed everything
  • From that point, recovery was much smoother
  • Today: dad says 90-95%, but Alfred says he looks completely normal — fully recovered

Recovery Timeline

  • Most patients on the ward were discharged in 7-10 days, sometimes 2 weeks
  • Alfred's dad stayed almost a month (due to the unrelated complication)
  • A few rare cases stayed months (severe/emergency cases)
  • Once discharged and able to move more at home, recovery accelerates
  • In hospital: basically in bed all day except short walks
  • At home: movement increases naturally

Key Takeaways for Caregivers

Emotional Reality

  • While you're in it, you don't have answers — you don't know what's happening, what will fix it, or whether recovery will fully happen
  • That uncertainty is terrifying — whatever you're feeling is completely valid
  • There were long stretches where his dad wasn't really aware of what was going on
  • He just wanted to go home, even when he wasn't ready
  • Recovery is not a straight line — there were real regressions
  • Despite all the twists: full recovery

Advocacy is Critical

  • Be there for rounds daily — shift changes mean people don't always know the full story
  • Someone needs to advocate consistently
  • Don't be afraid to intervene with the ward when something isn't working

Practical Tips

  • Hospitals are bright, loud, uncomfortable — bring things to make it feel more like home (music, small speaker, familiar shows, personal items)
  • Having someone around consistently helps — both for the patient and for staying on top of communication between doctors/nurses
  • Encourage walking once safe — not just waiting for physio, but small walks with family
  • Plan support so you're not carrying everything alone
  • Once discharged: having her stay with family (with help coming in) will likely speed recovery

Self-Care Reminder

  • Caregiving takes huge mental and emotional energy
  • Especially once home and nurses aren't there anymore
  • Take care of yourself — you can't pour from an empty cup
